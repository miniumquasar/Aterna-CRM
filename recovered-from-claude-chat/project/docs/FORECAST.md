# Forecast

**Forecast** (Practice → Forecast) shows where the work falls in the months ahead, and the money with it. Everything on it comes from the practice's own records: the deadlines, tasks, work in progress, the fees recorded on clients' services, and invoices and money received.

Staff see their own clients. Partners and managers see the whole practice, and can pick out one person's work.

## The choices at the top

Two choices scope everything on the screen, so the figures always agree with each other.

| Choice | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Whose work** | *Everyone's*, *Mine*, *Nobody's yet*, or one person. Deadlines go by their person (the deadline's own, or else the client's). Tasks and work in progress go by whom they are assigned to. Fees and money go by the client's person. Staff choose between *All my clients* and *Assigned to me*. |
| **Months ahead** | 3, 6 or 12, this month included. |

While new figures load, the old ones stay on screen, dimmed.

## The four figures

| Figure | Shows |
|---|---|
| **Overdue now** | Open deadlines already past their due date, and how late the oldest is. |
| **Due in the next … months** | Open deadlines falling due in the months shown. |
| **Busiest month** | The month with the most deadlines. |
| **Owed to the practice** | Money owed on invoices, and how much of it is overdue. *None recorded* until invoices are recorded in Billing. |

## Deadlines

**Deadlines falling due, by month** counts the same open deadlines as Compliance → Deadlines and Home, in the month each falls due. Overdue deadlines are counted in the figure at the top, not in a month. The tallest month is labelled. *Show the figures* lists every month as a table.

**What falls due, by kind of work** is a table with a cell for each kind of work and month. The lighter the cell, the more fall due that month; the scale under the table runs from fewer to more. Overdue ones sit in their own column with a warning sign, so they never look like part of the months.

**Who has what, by month** is the same table by person, with *Nobody yet* last. It shows only for partners and managers. While no client has a person, it says so and links to Clients, where clients are ticked and assigned.

## Tasks and work in progress

- **Tasks** counts open tasks: overdue, due this month, due later, and with no due date. Completed and cancelled tasks aren't counted. Staff see tasks for their own clients, and tasks given to them.
- **Work in progress** lists open jobs by the person doing them, with how many are late (past their deadline, or their due date when there is none) and how many are due this month. Completed, billed and cancelled jobs aren't counted.

## Billing expected

The fees recorded on clients' active services, placed in the months they are billed:

| How often | Placed |
|---|---|
| **Monthly** | Every month from the service's next date. With no next date, every month. |
| **Quarterly** | Every third month from the next date. A next date already past carries on in the same rhythm. |
| **Annually** | Once a year, in the month of the next date. |
| **One-off** | Once, in the month of its date, when that is one of the months shown. |
| **No next date** (quarterly or annual) | Spread evenly over the year. |
| **As needed, or no frequency** | Left out, and counted under the chart. |

A client whose services have no fees counts its annual fee instead, spread evenly over the year. A client with service fees never has its annual fee counted as well.

Money is worked out in whole pence. A yearly fee spread over the months is shared out so that any twelve months in a row add up to the fee exactly. No penny is lost to rounding.

While no fees are recorded, the section says how to add them: open a client, then *Services*.

## Money in

The figures are worked out exactly as in Billing:
- what is owed now;
- how much of it is overdue;
- what is held as credit;
- what is owed on invoices with no due date.

Under them, two charts on the same scale:
- **Received, by month**: money received in each month so far, this month included.
- **Falling due, by month**: what invoices still owe, by the month each falls due.

While no invoices are recorded, the section links to Billing.

## Briefing from the assistant

*Write a briefing* asks the assistant on this computer for a short briefing on the figures on screen. The briefing covers where the pressure falls, what is overdue, the money, and two or three suggestions. The assistant is given the figures on this screen and nothing else. *What the assistant was given* shows exactly that text, so the briefing can be checked against it.

The assistant can be wrong. It runs on this computer, and nothing is sent anywhere. [ASSISTANT.md](ASSISTANT.md) covers setting it up.
